> 
> This pattern echoes earlier financial bubbles rooted in major innovations in public finance — think the 1830s railroad boom or the 1920s stock market frenzy. In each case, investors mistook the trend — the expansion of infrastructure or the rise in speculative trading — for a direct path to profit. But spotting a big trend is far easier than mastering how to profit from it. The real winners weren’t always the pioneers who built the rails or the brokers who traded stocks; they were the suppliers, the engineers, the software developers, and the adjacent industries that fed the trend.
> 
> The railroad boom illustrates this perfectly: while railroads themselves became symbols of progress, the true economic engine was the ecosystem around them — the steel producers, the locomotive manufacturers, the freight logistics firms, and the real estate developers who built stations. These indirect beneficiaries thrived as the rail network expanded, while the railroads themselves often struggled financially until they were monetized through broader economic growth.

> Today, the internet’s future effects are vast and enduring — from e-commerce to cloud computing to AI-driven platforms. But the winners won’t be the pure-play internet companies.
